Transport and logistics

A haulage business has few cost blocks but large ones: vehicles, fuel, tolls and staff. Each is treated differently for tax, and cross-border journeys are treated differently again.

VAT on cross-border journeys
Transport services for businesses elsewhere in the EU are taxed in the customer's country. You need the customer's verified VAT identification number and the turnover goes into the EC sales list.
Vehicles and private use
Logbook or the one per cent rule. We work through both before you commit for a full year.
Payroll with subsistence allowances
Meal and overnight allowances stay tax free for the driver as long as the periods away from home are documented.
Tolls, fuel and repairs
Recorded as they arise, so that the input VAT on fuel cards and garage invoices is recovered in full.
Cash handling in the taxi trade
Cash takings fall under the cash register regulations. We tell you which records the tax office expects to see in an audit.
Depreciation of the fleet
Useful lives, payments on account for new vehicles and the disposal of older units in the annual accounts.

Common questions

Questions we hear often

Do I charge German VAT on a run to Poland?
For carriage on behalf of a business elsewhere in the EU, usually not. The customer accounts for the tax in their own country. This requires a verified VAT identification number, which we check before the first invoice.
How do I evidence the drivers' allowances?
Through the periods of absence. If route plans or digital tachograph data exist, the evidence is already there.
Is a logbook worth the effort?
With heavy business use and an expensive vehicle, almost always. With an older vehicle and a low list price, rarely. This can be calculated in advance.
